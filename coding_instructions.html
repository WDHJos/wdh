WDH Coding Instructions for AI and Student Assistants

This page provides instructions for the first evaluation of a publication uploaded for possible inclusion in the World Database of Happiness (WDH) bibliography. It is intended for use by AI and Student Assistants before the Notation Form is completed.

Purpose

The purpose of this procedure is to determine whether an uploaded scientific publication is relevant for the WDH bibliography and whether it may already be present in the existing publications list. The procedure also supports the selection of appropriate WDH subject descriptions.

Reference Pages

AI and Student Assistants must consult the following WDH website pages when evaluating a publication:

  • WDH Home
  • About the Bibliography
  • About the Publications
  • Publications
  • Subjects

Step 1 – Duplication Check

Before any content analysis, perform a duplication check against the WDH Publications page: publications.html.

The duplication check must be limited to the following two elements:

  • the title of the uploaded publication;
  • the main author, meaning the first author of the uploaded publication.

Use fuzzy comparison, because spelling differences, abbreviations, typing errors, translated titles, and formatting differences may occur in the publications table.

Report one of the following outcomes:

  • DUPLICATE LIKELY
  • POSSIBLE DUPLICATE
  • NO DUPLICATE FOUND

If a possible or likely duplicate is found, list the matching publication or publications and briefly explain the reason for the match. Do not use other criteria for this duplication check.

Step 2 – Determine Relevance for the WDH

Read the abstract of the uploaded publication. If no abstract is available, read the introduction. If the abstract or introduction does not provide sufficient information, analyze additional relevant sections of the publication.

To determine whether the publication belongs in the World Database of Happiness, consult: bibliography.html and about_publications.html.

Use the definitions, inclusion criteria, and philosophy of the WDH as described on those pages. The publication should be assessed in relation to happiness as the subjective appreciation of life as a whole.

If there is uncertainty about inclusion, AI must state the uncertainty explicitly and discuss the issue with the Student Assistant before continuing.

Step 3 – Content Summary

AI must provide a concise factual summary of the publication. The summary should include:

  • the main research question;
  • the population studied;
  • the main variables investigated;
  • the main findings;
  • the relevance for happiness research.

The Student Assistant reviews this summary together with AI before proceeding to subject selection.

Step 4 – Subject Proposal

AI must consult the WDH Subjects page: subjects.html.

Based on the content of the publication, AI proposes the most relevant WDH subject descriptions.

The subject proposal must:

  • use subject descriptions found on the WDH Subjects page;
  • include the corresponding Subject ID where available;
  • briefly explain why each subject was selected;
  • contain no more than 10 candidate subjects;
  • rank the subjects from most relevant to least relevant.

The final subject selection is made by the Student Assistant in consultation with AI.

Step 5 – Wait for the Notation Form

AI must not fill in the Notation Form during this first evaluation phase.

After the publication has been evaluated and the subjects have been discussed, the Student Assistant will upload a separate Notation Form together with a new prompt. Only then should AI proceed with coding and completion of the Notation Form.

Prompt for AI Use

The following instruction can be used when uploading a publication PDF:

Consult the WDH website before evaluating this publication: https://wdhjos.github.io/wdh/. First perform a fuzzy duplication check using only the title and the main author against the Publications page. Then assess whether the publication belongs in the WDH by consulting the Bibliography and About the Publications pages. Read the abstract, or the introduction if no abstract is available. If necessary, analyze more text from the PDF. Provide a short factual summary and propose relevant WDH subjects from the Subjects page. Do not complete the Notation Form until a separate Notation Form prompt is uploaded.
